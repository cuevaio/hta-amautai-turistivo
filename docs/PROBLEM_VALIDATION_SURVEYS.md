## Current version

Both surveys cover grades 1-6 of primary education in Peru. Second grade is a presentation demo case, not the product's scope limit. Eligibility is checked before applying the survey; question 1 offers the six primary grades only. The previous ineligible-respondent options were removed at the user's request.

Each form has seven survey questions followed by two adult contact fields: nine Google Forms items in total, all required. Names and telephone numbers remain mandatory and last. The user explicitly removed the former question 8 about contact authorization. Questions 1-7 and their online option labels are unchanged. Public titles and brief descriptions are neutral and contain no product name or invitation to try a product.

| Question | Purpose and response |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Primary grade, single choice |
| 2 | Frequency of needing additional help during the past four weeks, single choice |
| 3 | Main difficulty in the most recent occasion, single choice |
| 4 | One concrete example, required free text; write that no difficulty occurred when applicable |
| 5 | First action taken, single choice |
| 6 | Observed result, required free text; indicate no intervention or unknown result when applicable |
| 7 | Time spent supporting or seeking help on that occasion, single choice |

"Nombres y apellidos" and "Número de teléfono" are required short-answer fields for the adult respondent and remain the final two items. The form no longer asks for follow-up authorization. Contact details alone do not establish agreement to product promotion or participation in a future test.

Each form contains 33 option labels across five single-choice questions and two narrative questions. Question 7 uses the same time categories in both forms, including no additional time, no difficulty, and inability to recall.

The design follows The Mom Test: recent behavior and concrete experiences rather than hypothetical product approval. The frequency question uses a five-point ordinal scale plus an unobserved option. Completion time for the revised version has not been measured.

## Street interviews

Confirm eligibility verbally before starting. Interview only parents or guardians of primary students and teachers who teach primary mathematics, respectively. Exclude ineligible people from eligible-response denominators.

Read questions and choices neutrally, avoid pitching Amautai before the interview, and record the respondent's own words. Ask respondents to think about one child or the specified recent class. Keep one record per respondent and avoid counting a paper interview again when transcribing it online. A mandatory question can still have a valid answer indicating no difficulty, no extra support, or an unknown outcome.

## Pitch evidence

Report teachers and families separately, state counts as X of N respondents, and use real anonymized examples. Survey answers support problem discovery, not claims of learning improvement or product efficacy. Exclude names and telephone numbers from presentations. Providing contact details is not product adoption, willingness to pay, or verified product traction.

Treat the frequency scale as ordinal. Keep "No lo pude observar" separate from "Nunca"; use valid denominators. Treat time as reported categories, not a precise measured duration. Street recruitment is a convenience sample, so conclusions describe the respondents and should not be generalized to all Peruvian families. These questions do not directly validate contextualization, language needs, or demand for games.

## Results snapshot (October 18, 2026)

The response-linked Sheets contained 28 teacher submissions and 56 family submissions at the time of review. Among teachers, 18 of 28 (64.3%) selected frequently or always for students needing additional help in the last four weeks; 11 of 28 (39.3%) identified understanding what a problem asked as the main difficulty. Among families, 33 of 56 (58.9%) selected sometimes, frequently or always for needing help; 14 of 56 (25.0%) identified understanding what a problem asked as the main difficulty. These are separate question totals, not linked respondent-level findings.

The public page at `/lista-de-espera` lists only the names from the family and teacher response Sheets in separate columns. It excludes phone numbers and survey answers. This is a static snapshot and does not sync automatically with the private response Sheets. The convenience sample is not representative of Peru; these self-reports do not demonstrate product adoption, learning gains or Amautai efficacy.

## Product context and history

On October 17, the original two-section forms were merged into one page. The original seven-question wording was initially preserved. Later, the ineligible-respondent options were removed. An intermediate revision added time spent, converted the combined contact question into explicit consent, added two initially optional contact fields, and made the eight numbered questions required. A subsequent revision made both contact fields mandatory, kept them last, and shortened the descriptions. Superseded PDFs and screenshots do not describe the current version.

The latest neutral revision removes the former authorization question 8 from both forms and the family print copy, removes product branding and product-test invitations, and preserves questions 1-7 plus the two required contact fields. WhatsApp invitations describe the audience and recent mathematics experience without promising a product benefit.
